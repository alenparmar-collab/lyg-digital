# Ordinary Time

This is the design system for Lourdes Youth Group at CTM Parish. SKILL.md is written for Claude. This file is for the people who look after the design.

## The short version
Ordinary Time looks like a small independent zine printed on a risograph. There is warm paper, two flat inks that overlap, a bit of grain, big headlines and handwritten notes in the margins. One of the two inks changes with the Church season, so the website is green for most of the year, violet in Advent and Lent, gold at Christmas and Easter, and red at Pentecost. Lourdes blue stays the same all year.

## Why it looks like this
We wanted the registration to feel like something a young person would actually screenshot, and not like a church form moved online. Zine and riso printing already live in the places Gen Z spends time: gig posters, indie merch, Instagram and Pinterest. The look is handmade and a little imperfect, which reads as honest. That matters to people who have grown up surrounded by polished brand content.

The season colours are there for a deeper reason. The liturgical year walks through the life of Christ, from waiting for his birth to his death, resurrection and the sending of the Spirit. When the colours follow that calendar, Christ is the thing organising the design, not a picture placed on top of it. A member who joins in Lent gets a violet card and one who joins at Easter gets a gold one. People will compare, ask why, and learn something about the year without being lectured.

Lourdes blue is the constant because the group carries Our Lady of Lourdes in its name. The grotto arch is used to frame photos and the member card for the same reason.

## Symbols we use and why
The registration marks in the corners of each page are the crosshair circles printers use to line up their plates. They also happen to be crosses. It is a quiet sign that works for people who notice it and does not shout at people who don't.

The Lourdes rose recalls the roses at Our Lady's feet in Bernadette's vision. It appears as a stamp when someone joins.

We avoid doves, clip-art crosses, glowing light rays and stock photos of churches. They are what most youth ministry websites already use, and they make the faith look like decoration.

## Humour
The group is funny and the product should be too, within limits. Jokes live in the margins and are always about youth group life, such as the chairs, the samosas and the WhatsApp group. They never touch the Mass, Mary, prayer or anyone's faith, and they switch off during the Triduum. voice.md has examples.

## Keeping it healthy over time
Add new colours only as season inks, and only when the Church calendar gives a reason. If a screen starts to need a third ink, the screen probably has too much going on.

Photography should be of real members, taken with consent, and turned into duotone. Book a short photo session each year so the site keeps looking like the current group.

When someone new builds a page, ask them to read the quality gates at the end of SKILL.md before they start.

Privacy rules are in SKILL.md and are not optional. Many members are under 18, so check them again whenever the member card or data model changes.
